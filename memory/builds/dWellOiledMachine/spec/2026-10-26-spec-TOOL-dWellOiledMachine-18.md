## 1. Goal

Every wait gov holds today is `--hold` with a resume schedule, and it waits for a person
(`tools/unattended/unattended.sh:1191-1215`): one weekly-limit stall woke a session 115 times over 4.8
days, and eight run records still read HELD after their work landed (gap-rt-38). This unit gives a
wait a record of its own, keyed by the waited record's id and the build, woken when that record
arrives on the change feed, an owner request on a build no lease holds woken the same way, and one
sweep at the node tick, generic over each wait's declared deadline, that raises each wait past its
deadline exactly once (layer-runtime `[schedule]` and `[supervise]`, the stall-and-loop record's item
6). The release ceiling's arm is dShippedRelease's (the owner's FK-2-3).

## 2. Scope (IN)

- **S1** — The wait record (I29), `refs/gov/waits/<build>/<wait id>`, the wait id the first 12 hex of
  SHA-256 over `<kind> <record id> <build>`. Each state is one commit built by `build_gov_commit` and
  written by `write_gov_ref` (TOOL-dPlacedRequest-11 S1, S2), parented on the tip read, its message the
  header `gov-wait <build>/<wait id>`, a blank line and the keys `kind`, `record`, `build`, `on`,
  `after`, `deadline-seconds`, `outcome`, `state`, `fleet-tag` and `session`; `state` is `open`,
  `woken`, `raised` or `closed`, and a `woken`, `raised` or `closed` commit adds `by`, the commit that
  woke it, the sweep's session or the closing waiter's. Observed by AC1, AC2.
- **S2** — The declaration, `declare_wait(kind, record, build, on, seconds, outcome)` in
  `tools/runtime/waits.py` and
  `python waits.py declare <kind> <record id> <build> --on <ref> --seconds <n> [--outcome <name>]`:
  `on` is the ref the awaited record arrives at, every ref under a prefix when it ends in `/`, or
  `-` for a timed wait no record ends, and `after` its tip at the declaration, or `-` when no ref is
  there; a prefix's tip is the first 12 hex of SHA-256 over the sorted `<ref> <tip>` lines under it.
  A declaration on a key whose wait is not `open` appends a fresh `open` commit. A waiter that ends
  its own wait runs `python waits.py close <kind> <record id> <build>`, which writes `closed`. It
  prints the I29 line `wait — <kind> · <record id> · <build> · deadline <utc>`, the deadline the
  declaration's time plus the seconds. A second declaration of an `open` key prints
  `wait — already open · <wait id>` and exits 0; a kind outside `WAIT_KINDS` or an outcome outside
  `WAIT_OUTCOMES` is refused, exit 2, before any write. Observed by AC1, AC8.
- **S3** — The wake, in `sweep_waits(repo)` and `python waits.py sweep`: one bounded fetch of
  `refs/gov/waits/*` and of each open wait's `on` ref into the cache, then `add_feed_sightings`
  (TOOL-dPlacedRequest-13 S4); an `open` wait whose `on` tip differs from its `after` is written
  `woken` with `by` that tip, through a compare-and-swap on the tip read, and printed `wait — woken ·
  <kind> · <record id> · <build>`. A lost compare-and-swap re-reads and changes nothing. Observed by
  AC2, AC3.
- **S4** — The wake of a build no lease holds. A woken wait whose build lease, read through
  `unattended.sh --claims`, is absent, `released` or terminal runs its kind's `wake` from `WAIT_KINDS`
  once, under the cadence lease `refs/gov/leases/run/wake/<wait id>` (TOOL-dPlacedRequest-1 S1, the
  cadence `wake` a tag-grammar segment by TOOL-dPlacedRequest-18 S1) taken through `--lease acquire run
  wake <wait id>` and closed `--as landed` after it; a lease another node holds skips it. A build whose
  lease is live is its holder's: the position engine's `awaiting` answer reads the woken state.
  Observed by AC3.
- **S5** — The deadline, in the same sweep: each `open` wait whose declaration commit
  `check_deadline_passed <wait ref> <commit> <deadline-seconds>` (TOOL-dPlacedRequest-17 S4) reads
  passed, rc 0, is written `raised` through a compare-and-swap, and only the writer that wins runs the
  outcome. rc 1 and rc 2 leave it open, so a wait no feed has sighted is never raised. A `woken`,
  `raised` or `closed` wait is never raised again. An outcome `WAIT_OUTCOMES` marks `holder-only` is
  raised only by a sweep on the node whose fleet tag the wait names, since only its holder may write
  that build's leases. Observed by AC4, AC5, AC8.
- **S6** — The outcomes, `WAIT_OUTCOMES`, one row per name with its `holder-only` flag, shipping
  `owner-item` and `default`, neither holder-only: `owner-item` raises
  `raise_owner_item("wait-deadline", build, ["keep-waiting", "default"], "default", key=<wait id>)`
  (TOOL-dWellOiledMachine-48 S1), the kind `wait-deadline` joining `OWNER_ITEM_KINDS` in this unit;
  `default` writes the `raised` state only, which the waiter reads as its declared default taken.
  The sweep prints `wait — raised · <kind> · <record id> · <build> · <outcome>`. Observed by AC4.
- **S7** — The kinds, `WAIT_KINDS` in `waits.py`, one row per kind naming its default outcome and its
  `wake`, a function of the runtime kit or none. This unit ships `owner-request`, outcome
  `owner-item`, wake none until TOOL-dWellOiledMachine-3 sets it; each later kind joins in its own
  unit. Observed by AC1, AC3.
- **S8** — The node tick: `tools/unattended/resume-tick.sh` runs `waits.py sweep` once per tick after
  `owner_items.py decide`, so a wait raised in one tick has its item decided no earlier than the next;
  `--dry-run` prints what it would wake or raise and writes nothing; a failed sweep prints one line and
  leaves the tick's exit unchanged. Observed by AC6.
- **S9** — `help/runtime.md` gains a waits section: the record, the key, the wake, the one sweep, the
  outcomes and the release ceiling's arm left to dShippedRelease. Observed by AC7.
- **S10** — The fixture, `tools/runtime/waits_fixture.py --case <name>`: a bare remote and two clones
  under `%TEMP%` with short names, two fleet tags, the unattended kit resolved through
  `resolve_kit_dir`, and a recording `wake` registered for the case. It prints the verbs' last lines,
  the wait refs' states and the owner items posted. Observed by AC1 to AC6, AC8.

  coordination namespaces, and a wait carries no authority beyond raising one item.

## 4. Design

### Evidence

- The waits today are holds: `run_hold` (`tools/unattended/unattended.sh:6848`) files each under
  `resume_schedule_name` (:1191), whose fire rule (:1200-1215) wakes a session at an instant or after a
  delay, never on a record and never with a deadline past which anything else happens.
- No sweep over waits exists: `git grep -n "deadline" -- tools/unattended/resume-tick.sh` finds
  nothing, and the tick's jobs are the worktree scan (`resume-tick.sh:419`) and the resume.
- The layer names the wake's key and lease: "keyed by (event, the exit record's id, build)", run "under
  its instance's cadence lease, so it needs no build lease", and an owner request on a build whose
  lease is gone "is an event too" (layer-runtime.md:812-820).

### Data model

```
gov-wait fx/3f9a0c11d2e4

kind: owner-request
record: 7c1d02aa9b3e
build: fx
on: refs/gov/life/fx
after: 4e1f…
deadline-seconds: 259200
outcome: owner-item
state: open
fleet-tag: a
session: s1
```

The deadline is judged on the observer clock from the declaration commit's first sighting, so a
node that fetched the wait late judges it later, never earlier.

### Alternatives rejected

- **A node-local wait file.** A take-over on another node would not see the wait, and an owner request
  on a build no lease holds has no node to keep it.
- **A sweep per wait kind.** Each kind's ceiling would be one more loop at the tick, and the clause
  asks for one generic over each wait's declared deadline.
- **An outcome named as an argv in the record.** A ref any session can write would then name a
  command the tick runs; the outcome and the wake are names the kit's own tables map to code.

## 5. Production-readiness checklist

- security — the record carries names only: its outcome and wake resolve through the kit's closed
  tables, so no ref content becomes a command; writes go through the one ref writer.
- perf / scale — one bounded fetch per tick, one `check_deadline_passed` per open wait.
- error / empty / loading states — an unsighted wait is never raised; a lost compare-and-swap changes
  nothing; a fetch failure prints one line and leaves the tick's exit unchanged.
- observability — the `wait —` lines and the states on the ref, which the change feed carries.
- risks — a wait whose `on` ref moves for an unrelated reason wakes early; its waiter re-reads and may
  declare again.
- testing — the fixture cases under AC1 to AC6 and the arm in §7.
- migration — none: no wait record exists today; holds keep their schedules until
  TOOL-dWellOiledMachine-28 dissolves them.
- user docs — `help/runtime.md`.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

Each case runs `python waits_fixture.py --case <name>` in its scratch clones.

- **AC1** — When `--case declare` declares an `owner-request` wait twice for one record and build, the
  first prints `wait — owner-request · <record id> · fx · deadline <utc>`, the second `wait — already
  open`, and the remote holds one ref under `refs/gov/waits/fx/`; an unknown kind exits 2 and writes
  nothing.
  Red when: a second ref is written for one key.
- **AC2** — When `--case wake` writes a commit at the wait's `on` ref and runs `waits.py sweep`, the
  wait reads `woken` with `by` that commit; a sweep with `on` unchanged leaves it `open`.
  Red when: a wait is woken with its record unchanged, or stays open after it arrives.
- **AC3** — When `--case unleased-request` declares an `owner-request` wait on a build with no build
  lease and writes the request at `on`, the first sweep runs the recording wake once under
  `refs/gov/leases/run/wake/<wait id>` and the second runs nothing; with the build lease live on
  another node, no wake runs.
  Red when: the wake runs twice, never, or under a held build.
- **AC4** — When `--case owner-request-past` plants an `owner-request` wait with `--seconds 1`, sleeps
  past it and runs `waits.py sweep` three times, exactly one owner item `owner item wait-deadline fx
  <wait id>` is posted and the wait reads `raised`.
  Red when: the planted owner-request wait past its deadline is raised twice, as a sweep with its
  `raised` check removed and its item keyed by the run's time shows, or never.
- **AC5** — When `--case two-nodes` runs the sweep from both clones at once over one wait past its
  deadline, one wins the compare-and-swap, one item is posted, and the loser prints nothing raised.
  Red when: both nodes raise the wait.
- **AC6** — When `bash tools/unattended/resume-tick.sh --dry-run` runs in the fixture clone with one
  wait past its deadline, it prints `wait — raised` as a would-be line and the ref still reads `open`.
  Red when: the dry run writes the state, or the tick runs no sweep.
- **AC8** — When `--case closed-and-holder` closes one timed wait (`--on -`) before its deadline and
  plants a second past it whose outcome the case marks `holder-only` with fleet tag `b`, a sweep from
  tag `a` leaves both as they are and a sweep from tag `b` raises only the second.
  Red when: a closed wait is raised, or a holder-only outcome runs on another node.
- **AC7** — When `grep -n "refs/gov/waits" runtime.md` runs over the help page, it names the record,
  and the page names `waits.py sweep` and the release ceiling's arm as dShippedRelease's.
  Red when: the record, the sweep or the hand-over is absent.
