## What does not fit, stated rather than forced

**Section 4 is filled by NEITHER, and that is the finding.** Both references encode where their
output lands in prose a machine cannot read. It is why the checklist reference's own checker can
validate a plan row and cannot validate that a piece landed anywhere, and it is exactly the gap
units 4, 5 and 6 exist to close. A canon derived purely by generalising the two would not contain
this section at all.

**Section 8 exists in one reference as INVARIANTS and in neither as a SECTION.** The checklist
reference carries a read-the-previous-piece pass plus byte-level and heading-level repetition
invariants and a share ceiling — four set-scoped controls, three of them machine checks — and no
heading gathers them. Promoting them to a required section is the canon's one substantive addition
to what the corpus already knew.

**Sections 6 and 9 are `none — <why>` in one reference each.** That is what the declared-null rule
is for. Forcing the recipe reference to invent gate legs, or the checklist reference to invent a
producer scaffold, would have been the distortion the audit warned about when it asked whether
twelve was the right size.

**The recipe reference has no step-shaped lines under the checklist reference's selector**, and nine
under its own. This is why `step_selector` is DECLARED per playbook and carries a shrink-only floor:
a kit-fixed selector reports "every step is tagged" over an empty set on one of the two files the
canon came from.

## The frozen mark

The canon ships FROZEN with a `curated` line, per fork 4. It was derived from this corpus and from
the external instruction-design record in this folder, then edited by hand — a derived table nobody
edited is a mirror of its subject, which is the one shape a template must not have.

## What this record does NOT establish

Neither reference was re-read for this mapping; it rests on the corpus-anatomy record, whose own
limits that record states. No playbook has been written from the canon yet, so the claim that both
references FIT it is a mapping claim rather than an observed round-trip. The honest test is the
first playbook authored from the template, and unit 11 is where that happens.
