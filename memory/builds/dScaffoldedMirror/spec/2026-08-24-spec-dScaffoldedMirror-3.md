## 1. Goal

`lexicon.py:125-129` is a bare `git ls-files` with no exclusion, so the kit grades and seeds itself.
Ten of this repo's 463 verb offenders sit in `tools/lexicon/`, and `tracked`, `ext`, `python`,
`probe` and `glob` — the exact non-verbs that bled into the owner's demo proposal — are that kit's
own leading tokens. Scope the graded corpus from the adopter's own install receipt, print what was
excluded on every run, and refuse rather than guess where no receipt exists. A derived proposal
computed over a corpus containing the deriver is untrustworthy on arrival, which makes this a
prerequisite for every other derivation rather than a phase of its own.

## 2. Scope (IN)

- **S1** — the exclusion is READ from `.governance/install.json`, the receipt `govkit apply` writes
  (`govkit.py:2819-2830`). Only rows whose `role` is `engine` or `diverged` are excluded: those are
  bytes gov owns and the adopter did not author. `seed`, `project-owned` and `generated` rows stay
  graded, because the adopter wrote them.
- **S2** — every run prints one corpus line, on green as well as on red:
  `corpus — graded N of M tracked file(s) · excluded K by <rule>`. A shrink of the graded set is
  never invisible.
- **S3** — a receipt row naming a path that is not tracked REDS, naming the row. This is the
  `UNSELECTIVE LAYERS RULE` law (`lexicon.py:363-388`) applied to the second selector this kit
  acquires: a rule that selects nothing cannot fire, and a stale exclusion silently widens the
  surface it was written to narrow.
- **S4** — the excluded fraction is capped and exceeding it REDS. The cap is on the excluded share
  of the graded DEFINITION population, not of tracked files, and is set at 25%; §4 derives that
  number.
- **S5** — where no receipt exists, the DERIVING verb refuses by name and the CHECKING verb does
  not. `bash tools/lexicon/adopt-lexicon.sh --scaffold` exits 1 naming `.governance/install.json` as
  the missing input. `--check` prints `corpus — no receipt; grading all M tracked file(s), no
  exclusion derivable` and grades the whole tree.
- **S6** — `tools/lexicon/README.md` and `tools/lexicon/LEXICON.md` state what scoping does NOT do:
  it does not decide whether a file is worth grading, it reads no authored glob, and it cannot tell
  an authored kit from a vendored one without the receipt.

## 3. Non-goals (OUT)

- **No `CORPUS_GLOBS` key, and no authored exclusion of any spelling.** An adopter-authored path
  list is a pin with wildcards: it pre-absorbs every future file under the glob, silently. The
  exclusion has exactly one source and that source is generated by the installer.
- **No second receipt schema.** `install.index` is `incms`'s own generated spelling with its own
  columns. Reading two receipt formats is the `two-answers-to-one-question` class; §8 F2 resolves it
  and names the consequence.
- **No change to any predicate, pin, verb table or waiver key.** P1, P2 and P3 grade exactly what
  they grade today, over whatever corpus survives. Waiver keying is `TOOL-dScaffoldedMirror-4`.
- **No per-predicate reporting.** That is `TOOL-dScaffoldedMirror-2`, which lands first so the
  corpus line and the population line are two separate statements rather than one fold.
- **No coverage fraction.** `TOOL-dScaffoldedMirror-6` owns the graded-share-of-definition-carrying
  question; this unit owns the graded-share-of-tracked-files one.

## 4. Design

### The research rule is wrong on this repo, measured

Recommendation R2 states the default rule as "a file carrying a `# gov:kit <id>@<ver>` marker, or
living under a directory holding a `kit.toml`". Applied to this worktree at `af4de2d5` it selects
13 `kit.toml` directories holding 148 tracked files, plus 5 more files carrying an anchored marker
outside them — 153 of 899 tracked files, 17.0%. Because those directories hold the Python, the
effect on the GRADED population is not 17%:

| | graded definitions | verb offenders |
|---|---|---|
| excluded by the R2 rule | 670 | 381 |
| surviving | 154 | 82 |
| total | 824 | 463 |

That rule drains 82.3% of the pin in one commit and leaves nine files graded. It is the largest
absorbing move in this kit's history wearing a fix's clothes: the pin would fall 463 → 82 with a
green bar and a commit message about scoping.

The reason is structural rather than a tuning error. This repo AUTHORS the kits. A marker and a
`kit.toml` are what a COPY carries, and the original carries them too — that is what a copy is. No
predicate over a file's own bytes separates the author from the vendor.

### The discriminator that can

The receipt can, because it records an INSTALL rather than a shape. `govkit apply` writes
`.governance/install.json` with one row per written file carrying `path`, `role` and `sha256`, plus
a flat `install.sums` sidecar. `role` already carries the ownership answer this unit needs, and it
is govkit's own vocabulary rather than a new one: `engine` and `diverged` mean gov owns the bytes;
`seed`, `project-owned` and `generated` mean the target does.

Measured 2026-08-24: this worktree's `.governance/` holds `deploy.toml` and nothing else. There is
no receipt here because nothing was installed here. Under S1 this repo therefore excludes ZERO files
and goes on grading all 463 of its own offenders, which is the correct answer — they are its own
naming debt, not somebody else's engine.

Measured the same day, read-only, on the one real adopter: `C:/projects/incms/main` carries 92
receipt rows, of which 76 are `engine` or `diverged`, of which 29 are `.py` or `.js` — the armed
extensions. Against its 1,190 armed files that is 2.4%.

### The cap, and why 25%

The cap answers "how much of the graded population may an exclusion remove before it stops being an
exclusion". Two measurements bracket it. The only real receipt in existence removes 2.4% of the
armed population. The rule this spec rejects removes 82.3%. A cap at 25% leaves ten-fold headroom
above the observed adopter and trips on the refuted rule by a factor of three.

That is the whole justification, and it is the shape a threshold should have: the number sits where
the design we know is wrong fails it. It is a kit constant, not a conf key — a cap an adopter can
raise is the pin this build exists to delete, one indirection down.

### Where the refusal binds, and where it does not

The refusal is asymmetric because the defect is. An unscoped corpus makes a DERIVED PROPOSAL wrong:
the frequency ranking promotes the vendored engine's own tokens into the adopter's vocabulary, which
is the measured demo failure. An unscoped corpus makes the GATE stricter, not looser: it grades
files the adopter did not write and reports more offenders than it should. Those are different
defects and only the first is unsafe.

So `--scaffold` refuses without a receipt and `--check` does not. A `--check` that refused would
make the kit unusable in a hand-wired repo — the population that is every live adoption but one —
and un-adoption is the failure mode this kit can least afford.

### Data model

```
receipt row  {"path": "<repo-relative>", "role": "engine|diverged|seed|project-owned|generated"}
excluded     {row.path for row in receipt.files if row.role in ("engine", "diverged")}
corpus       [f for f in tracked_files(root) if f not in excluded]
```

The reader is `json.loads` plus a role filter, roughly twenty lines, and it does NOT import govkit.
That is not tidiness. `LAYERS` already declares that this kit ships self-contained so an adopter
taking it without another kit gets a working kit, and govkit is the deployer — never installed into
a target, so its code is not there to import. The receipt is a file FORMAT, and reading a format is
the one form of coupling that survives the install boundary.

A receipt that is present and unparseable REFUSES by name rather than degrading to "no exclusions".
Degrading would be safe by direction, since a larger corpus is stricter, but it is a silent state
change in a unit whose entire product is that a shrink is never invisible.

### Files touched (estimate)

`tools/lexicon/lexicon.py` (~80 lines: the receipt reader, the corpus filter, the corpus line, the
cap, the stale-row refusal), `tools/lexicon/adopt-lexicon.sh` (the `--scaffold` refusal),
`tools/lexicon/selftest.py` (the arms in §6), `tools/lexicon/README.md` and
`tools/lexicon/LEXICON.md` (S6). No conf grammar change, so `lexicon_conf.py` is untouched.

### Alternatives rejected

- **The R2 rule as written.** Measured above at 82.3% of this repo's pin. Kept as a frozen selftest
  fixture asserting that number, so a later session re-proposing it meets the measurement rather
  than the argument.
- **Excluding every non-adopter role.** `project-owned` and `seed` files are adopter-authored;
  excluding them would exempt exactly the files an adopter is most likely to name badly.
- **Reading `install.sums` instead of `install.json`.** The sidecar carries paths and hashes and no
  role, so it cannot make the author-versus-vendor distinction that is the whole design.

## 5. Production-readiness checklist

- **security** — the receipt is adopter-supplied JSON read by a checker that may run in CI. It is
  parsed with `json.loads` and never evaluated; only `files[].path` and `files[].role` are read;
  paths are used as set members compared against `git ls-files` output and are never opened, joined
  or resolved, so a `../` row can exclude nothing it does not literally name.
- **perf / scale** — one file read and one set difference. Observed receipt sizes are 92 rows on
  `incms` and absent on gov. The check is 0.44 s warm and this adds no measurable time.
- **a11y** — N/A, a CLI checker with no interactive surface.
- **i18n** — N/A. Paths and role tokens are ASCII by the receipt's own schema.
- **error / empty / loading states** — three states, each with its own message: no receipt (the S5
  split), a receipt that does not parse (refuse by name), and a receipt whose rows all name absent
  paths (S3 reds every row). An empty `files` array reds under S3's zero-match law.
- **observability** — the corpus line is the observability change, and it prints on green.
- **risks** — the real risk is the inverse of the usual one. This unit can only SHRINK a corpus, and
  a shrink drains a pin. On this repo it shrinks nothing, because there is no receipt, so the pin
  cannot move here. On an adopter it can, and the cap plus the printed count are what make that
  visible; the drain is bounded at 25% by construction rather than by review attention.
- **testing + left-shift gates** — six arms in `tools/lexicon/selftest.py` (§6). The classes are
  `memory/gotchas/allowlist-narrower-than-the-root-it-guards.md` for the cap and
  `memory/gotchas/vacuous-selector-empty-population.md` for the stale row.
- **migration / rollback** — none. No persisted artifact changes shape and no conf key is added or
  removed, so reverting the commit restores the previous corpus exactly.
- **user docs** — S6. The kit README gains the receipt paragraph and the explicit negative.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

- **AC1** — When `python tools/lexicon/lexicon.py` runs on this worktree, whose `.governance/` holds
  only `deploy.toml`, it prints a corpus line naming `no receipt` and the `verb` offender count is
  unchanged at `463`. Observed with no staged break.
- **AC2** — When the same command runs against a fixture repo carrying a `.governance/install.json`
  whose rows are `engine`, the corpus line names the excluded count and no excluded file contributes
  an offender. Asserted by a `selftest.py` arm, not by reading output.
- **AC3** — When a receipt row names a path absent from `git ls-files`, the run exits 1 and the
  message names that row's `path`. Staged in the fixture; there is no receipt in this tree to break.
- **AC4** — When a fixture receipt excludes more than 25% of the graded definition population, the
  run exits 1 naming the measured fraction and the cap. The fixture is built from the rejected R2
  rule's real numbers, so the arm asserts `82.3` against the cap constant.
- **AC5** — When `bash tools/lexicon/adopt-lexicon.sh --scaffold` runs in a repo carrying a vendored
  kit and no `.governance/install.json`, it exits 1 naming that path, and no `.lexicon.conf` is
  written.
- **AC6** — When `python tools/lexicon/lexicon.py` runs against a fixture whose receipt is present
  but malformed, it exits 1 naming the parse failure rather than reporting zero exclusions.

## 7. Gates

Keeps green: `lexicon naming predicates`, `lexicon selftest`, `lexicon wiring`, `memory hygiene`,
`kit version markers`. Adds no new gate leg — S3, S4, S5 and the malformed-receipt refusal are new
refusals inside the existing `lexicon naming predicates` leg, and the arms ride `lexicon selftest`.
The leg count is not the coverage.

## 8. Open questions

- **F1 — does the exclusion apply to `--check`, or only to the deriving verbs?** Applying it to both
  means an adopter's pin drops on the commit that lands this unit, by however many offenders their
  vendored engine holds. Applying it only to `--scaffold` leaves the gate grading files the adopter
  cannot fix. RECOMMENDATION: both, with the cap and the printed count as the controls, because a
  gate reporting offenders in files its reader may not edit trains that reader to ignore it.
  RESOLVED (agent, 2026-08-24, delegated): both, bounded by the 25% cap in §4.
- **F2 — does the reader accept `incms`'s `.governance/install.index` spelling as well?** It is the
  only receipt that exists on a real adopter, it carries the same role vocabulary, and refusing it
  drops the one hand-wired adopter into the S5 no-receipt branch. RECOMMENDATION: no. It is
  generated by `incms`'s own `scripts/check_kit_sync.py` with its own columns and its own
  `kits.json` population authority, and a kit reading two receipt schemas has two answers to one
  question — the class this build is named after. The consequence is stated rather than hidden: on
  `incms` today this unit prints the no-receipt line, which is correct, because `incms` has not
  adopted the lexicon at all. RESOLVED (agent, 2026-08-24, delegated): `install.json` only; the
  second spelling is a question for `TOOL-dScaffoldedMirror-15`, which owns telling adopters this
  kit exists.

## 10. Reuse audit

`python tools/codebase-map/reuse_lookup.py corpus scoping receipt exclusion vendored` returns
`registry.toml [govkit]` as an affordance seam matched on `receipt`, `corpus_files`
(`tools/memory-recall/extract.py`, fan-in 2), and the gotcha class
`pin-copied-from-another-corpus.md`. The govkit hit is the right seam and it is the one this unit
may NOT wire through: govkit owns the receipt's schema and its reader (`govkit.py:1487-1505`), but
govkit is the deployer and is never installed into a target, so an adopter holding a receipt does
not hold that code. The kit's own `LAYERS` philosophy — ship self-contained, port rather than import
— is the recorded reason, and it is why S1 specifies a twenty-line `json.loads` reader over the
receipt's two stable fields. `corpus_files` builds a memory-tree file list from declared sources and
shares no shape with a tracked-file filter. No existing seam fits; what is reused is the receipt
FORMAT, which is the only coupling that survives the install boundary.
