# aHonedRuleset — measure what is actually redundant in the governing prose, before trimming any of it

## The problem this build exists to solve
An instruction-optimization pass was asked for over this repo's load-bearing protocols, skills and
their templates. Nobody knows what in them is actually redundant. Two cheap theses were available and
neither had been tested: that the rendered guides have drifted from the kit templates they ship from,
and that the corpus is thick with copy-pasted rules. Meanwhile five carriers sit within 207 bytes of a
ceiling and three of them within twelve, so the next rule that must land in any of them is blocked on
a trim nobody has scoped. `TOOL-dFoldedVerdict-7` recorded that pattern from three carriers found in
one build and called it a pattern rather than a coincidence; its population was never enumerated.

## Expected improvements
- The divergence question is answered by measurement rather than by reading 420 KB.
- The corpus's real redundancy class is named, and the cut list is ranked by measured section weight.
- The ceiling pattern `TOOL-dFoldedVerdict-7` recorded gets its full measured population.
- Every figure re-derives on demand, so the report cannot rot into a stale census.

## Detriments if this is not built
- Trims land by taste, on whichever file a session happens to have open.
- The edit-locked carriers stay locked, and the next rule landing in one is funded by a guess.
- `TOOL-aScouredKit-23`'s uncapped-document finding stays at the two files it happened to name.

## Build-level rules
- **The census measures and edits no protocol prose.** Cuts are separate units the owner selects
  from the ranked list. A measurement pass that starts trimming has stopped being a measurement.
- **The report cites no figure the shipped script does not emit**, names the command that emits it,
  and says outright that the output wins when the two disagree. A dated measurement quoted with its
  derivation is not the thing §7 forbids; a number typed beside the thing it counts, with no way
  back to the measurement, is. A census whose findings were unquotable would be unreadable, so the
  rule is traceability rather than silence — and it binds hardest here, on a document whose entire
  subject is documents.
- **Authored sources only.** Five of the six named documents are rendered from kit templates and
  byte-compared by parity legs. A cut is made in the template and rendered down; hand-editing the
  copy under `memory/guides/` reds the leg and loses the edit at the next render.
- **The script states what it cannot see, where it reports.** Shingle matching finds copied text and
  is blind to a rule restated in different words, which is the costly half. A pair reported at zero
  shared bytes is not a pair with no duplicated meaning, and the header says so.

## Parked decisions
- **Whether `BUILD-METHOD`'s budget becomes a declared row.** The file sets its own `≤24 KB` in
  prose, says at its line 16 that no gate enforces the pair, and sits twelve bytes under it. Making
  it a `tools/template-size-limits.txt` row is a one-line change with an owner call behind it, and
  it belongs to whoever rules on the uncapped set rather than to this census.
- **Whether the uncapped documents get ceilings.** `TOOL-aScouredKit-23` owns that question and
  names two files; the census measures the real count. Raising it here would answer someone else's
  open row from inside a measurement pass.
- **Which cuts land, and in what order.** The ranked list is input to that decision, not the
  decision. The proposed-drop list is deliberately separate because dropping a rule is an owner call
  and merging the two lists would smuggle it into a byte-recovery exercise.
