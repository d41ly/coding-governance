The item cycle: six steps every role item follows, from next naming the item to the driver persisting the return and asking next again

Step 1 of 6 · the next engine, a command

next names the item and its actor

Reads

Witnesses only: committed records, git, leases and the mailbox. Never a remembered position.

Hands on

One checklist item, its actor, its allowance and its witness key.

Step 2 of 6 · the driver: the main session, or the executor when unattended

The driver briefs the prompt writer

Hands over

The item's definition and acceptance, references to its input records, its allowance, and the actor's return template.

Never

Performs the item. It holds only cards, typed returns and cheap state reads.

Step 3 of 6 · the prompt writer, a fresh role agent

The prompt writer composes the task

Returns

A tight task prompt, saved as a record and passed on unchanged. Same-role tasks are batched (prompts.batch).

Never

Dispatches the task, or performs it.

Step 4 of 6 · the executor

The executor spawns the actor fresh

Wraps

A work order: the item, its allowance, its return template, its witness and the hashes.

Spawns

A fresh agent whose system prompt is the role's static instructions. The always-spawn harness is owed to the lower-level step.

Step 5 of 6 · the actor, one of the role workers

The actor works and returns a typed template

Works inside

Its allowance: what it may read, write and run.

Returns

Its filled template through the schema-forced channel, ending in an outcome from a closed set. A value that fails to resolve is retried up to roles.retries, 3 by default.

Step 6 of 6 · the driver

The driver resolves the witness and persists

Does

Resolves the item's witness, persists the return with its hash, updates the card's pointers and reads the build's mailbox.

Then

Asks next again. No worker carries anything over; whatever a later item needs is in a record.
