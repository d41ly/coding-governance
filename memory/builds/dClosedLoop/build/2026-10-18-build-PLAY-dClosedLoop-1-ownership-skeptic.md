# Ownership re-verified: the disputed owners put to a skeptic

**Serves:** research PLAY-dClosedLoop-1

The owner asked three independent agents to re-verify PM-10's ownership table. In round 2 three verifiers each
derived an owner for every node from a worktree pinned at `a169b3350`, the table removed
(`build/2026-10-17-build-PLAY-dClosedLoop-1-ownership-verify.md` `[task-2]`). For 23 nodes, two or three of them
name an owner other than the approved one. By the disposition set before they ran, a skeptic tries to refute each
disagreement, and what survives goes to the owner. This record holds the skeptic's task, as `[task]`, and later its
verdicts.

## [task] The task the skeptic is handed

**Your findings** are the 23 rows of `[findings]` below. Each says: the approved owner of this node is wrong, and the
disputing verifiers' owner is right. For each one, try hard to refute it, which here means showing that the
approved owner is right.

**The rule both sides applied.** A node's owner is the one chain build that implements what the node names or
switches; a build that only calls, reads or runs it is not its owner. For each kind:
- an item: the build that builds the item's program step;
- a policy key: the build that implements the behaviour the key switches;
- an extension point: the build that builds the point's registration;
- a tag namespace: the build that registers the namespace with its extractor;
- a system: the build its own record names as its owner;
- a record kind: the build that registers the kind, with its schema and its writer, through its layer's contract.
  dTotalRecall builds the registry and the `record` command, and that alone does not make it every kind's owner.

**Rows the owner settled:** PM-9 gives the entry step's items to dDeclaredLoop and `entry.intake`'s hook to
dWellOiledMachine; RT-3 gives the lifecycle step's items to dWellOiledMachine; RC-8 gives `placement` and
`placement.check` to dDeclaredLoop.

**Read the records as they are now,** in the working tree under `memory/builds/dClosedLoop/build/`. The verifiers
read them at `a169b3350`, and two edits have landed since: the re-cut (`2026-10-18-build-PLAY-dClosedLoop-1-recut.md`
`[applied]` and `[reconciled]`), which moved the intake hook from dRankedPlan's row to dWellOiledMachine's, and the
order review's fold (`[order-review]`), which added a sixteenth build, dGroundRules. A dispute that rests on a row
those edits changed is refuted by the change; cite it. v2 is `2026-10-14-build-PLAY-dClosedLoop-1-architecture-v2.md`,
and its `[chain]` table's planned-units cells say what each build builds.

**Refute a finding** when the current records show that the approved owner implements what the node names or
switches, or that the disputing build only calls, reads or runs it, or that the dispute rests on a changed row.
**Confirm it** when the records show the disputing verifiers' owner implements it. **Mark it unverified** when the
records do not settle it either way, and say what would.

**Return** one table, one row per finding: the finding's id; its node; your verdict (confirmed, refuted or
unverified); the owner you would keep; and the reason you observed, as a file and line with the deciding words
quoted. Then one line of counts. Write nothing in the repository, and keep any scratch file in a folder of your own
inside the session scratchpad, never at its root.
